## The Psychology Of Trust In AI: A Guide To Measuring And Designing For User Confidence
- URL: https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2025/09/psychology-trust-ai-guide-measuring-designing-user-confidence/
- Type: article (guide)
- Date/author: 19 Sep 2025; Victor Yocco
- Summary:
  - Treats trust as a measurable construct with four pillars: ability, benevolence, integrity, predictability/reliability.
  - Describes a spectrum from active distrust through suspicion to calibrated trust, with over-trust (automation bias) at the far end.
  - Trust depends on how errors are handled (acknowledge, make correction easy), not on having none.
  - Warns against "trustwashing": making a flawed system look trustworthy.
  - Lists four validated scales: Trust in Automation Scale (Jian, Bisantz & Drury 2000), TOAST (Wojton et al. 2020), Trust in Automation Questionnaire (Körber 2018), Human Computer Trust Scale (Gulati, Sousa & Lamas 2019).
- Actionable takeaways: measure trust with a validated scale plus behaviour (correction rate, verification, disengagement); design error recovery as a trust feature.
- Psychology links: Calibrated Trust — the article's target state; Automation Bias — named as over-trust; Mental Model — predictability lets users form a stable model; Feedback Loop — error acknowledgement.
- New concepts: trust repair; trustwashing.

## Should You Actually Add AI To That? A Four-Question Audit For Product Teams
- URL: https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2026/11/should-you-add-ai-audit-product-teams/
- Type: article
- Date/author: 12 Nov 2026; Victor Yocco
- Summary:
  - Four questions: does it cut total effort including the checking users inherit; does it beat the current method; can the user catch the errors; can they still tell when the AI is wrong after a year.
  - Cites Cowan (2001) on the ~4-item working-memory limit, Parasuraman & Manzey (2010) on automation bias, Dratsch et al. (2023) on radiologists following wrong AI suggestions, Ackerhans et al. (2025) on professional identity threat, Rogers (2003) on pro-innovation bias.
- Actionable takeaways: remove AI from steps where the current method is already fast and reliable; test with wrong outputs.
- Psychology links: Automation Bias — core of question 3; Calibrated Trust — question 4; Cognitive Load — verification is added load; Miller's Law — ~4 chunks.
- New concepts: pro-innovation bias; professional identity threat (1 source each).

## Designing For Agentic AI: Practical UX Patterns For Control, Consent, And Accountability
- URL: https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2026/02/designing-agentic-ai-practical-ux-patterns/
- Type: article
- Date/author: 11 Feb 2026; Victor Yocco
- Summary: six patterns, each with a metric — Intent Preview (acceptance ratio), Autonomy Dial (setting churn), Explainable Rationale ("why?" tickets), Confidence Signal (calibration score), Action Audit & Undo (reversion rate), Escalation Pathway (recovery success rate).
- Actionable takeaways: preview before acting; make every agent action reversible within a stated window; show confidence.
- Psychology links: Calibrated Trust — Confidence Signal; Positive Friction — Intent Preview; Feedforward — preview of outcome; Provide Exit Points — undo; Reactance — Autonomy Dial keeps control with the user.

## Persuasive Design: Ten Years Later
- URL: https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2026/03/persuasive-design-ten-years-later/
- Type: article
- Date/author: 9 Mar 2026; Anders Toxboe
- Summary: behavioural design works as strategy, not isolated tweaks; game mechanics need intrinsic value (Self-Determination Theory); prompts cannot fix missing capability or opportunity (COM-B alongside the Fogg model); treat ideas as testable hypotheses with an ethics check.
- Psychology links: Goal Gradient Effect; Social Proof; Progressive Disclosure; Loss Aversion; Default Bias (status quo); Hyperbolic Discounting (present bias); Nudge.
- New concepts: COM-B (capability, opportunity, motivation) — 1 source.

## Designing For Distressed Users: Why Mental Health Apps Shouldn't Follow Every UI Fashion
- URL: https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2026/07/designing-distressed-users-mental-health-apps-ui/
- Type: article
- Date/author: 9 Jul 2026; Kat Homan
- Summary: anxiety, depression and burnout reduce the capacity to plan and choose; novel patterns demand learning users cannot afford; streaks can turn motivation into guilt. Proposes a five-point check for any UI trend: cognitive load, emotional alignment, navigational reliability, accessibility, engagement integrity.
- Psychology links: Stress-Narrowed Cognition; Cognitive Load; Familiarity Bias — predictable navigation; Variable Reward / streak mechanics — guilt risk.

## Matching AI Modality To User Intent: Designing The Right Interface
- URL: https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2026/07/matching-ai-modality-user-intent-designing-right-interface/
- Type: article
- Date/author: 2 Jul 2026; Victor Yocco
- Summary: chat is not the right interface for every AI task; choose input and output modality from the user's physical, social and cognitive constraints. Tools: a Task Audit and an input/output alignment matrix. No academic citations.
- Psychology links: Cognitive Load; Recognition Over Recall — visible options beat a blank prompt; Hick's Law — choice paralysis at an empty chat box; Stress-Narrowed Cognition — environment and load.

## Designing For Stress And Emergency
- URL: https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2025/11/designing-for-stress-emergency/
- Type: article
- Date/author: 24 Nov 2025; Vitaly Friedman
- Summary: under emergency stress people tunnel, read less and decide hastily or freeze; guidance: treat stress cases as core, one action at a time, sub-tasks of 10–30 seconds, clear status, safe defaults, undo, an emergency mode, and testing in real noisy conditions. Most psychological claims are uncited.
- Psychology links: Stress-Narrowed Cognition; Default Bias; Positive Friction — undo and safeguards; Chunking — short sub-tasks; Feedback Loop — clear status.

## Designing Friction For A Better User Experience
- URL: https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2018/01/friction-ux-design-tool/
- Type: article
- Date/author: 10 Jan 2018; Zoltan Kollin
- Summary: friction helps when it prevents irreversible errors, protects accounts and purchases, signals thoroughness, or changes behaviour for the user's benefit; excessive friction in cancellation flows is a dark pattern.
- Psychology links: Positive Friction; Labor Illusion — deliberate delays that raise trust (Coinstar, security checks); Nudge; Reactance.

## Design Guidelines For Better Notifications UX
- URL: https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2025/07/design-guidelines-better-notifications-ux/
- Type: article
- Date/author: 7 Jul 2025; Vitaly Friedman
- Summary: frequency is the main complaint; tier notifications by severity; start with a low default frequency; offer modes, summaries, snooze and quiet hours; a Facebook experiment found fewer notifications improved satisfaction and long-term usage.
- Psychology links: Alert Fatigue; External Trigger; Self-Initiated Triggers — user-set modes and timing; Reactance; Default Bias.
